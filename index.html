What are the rings?

Every joint that can bend has a ring around it. Each ring is a tiny gauge: it fills up and turns from green to orange to red as that joint curls.

To measure a bend you need three dots — the joint plus its two neighbours. We measure the corner between them. Straight is 180°, fully curled is about 30°.

15 joints per hand bend (3 per finger). The wrist and the five fingertips don't, so they have no ring.

Why are there more dots than landmarks?

The camera only ever finds 21 dots per hand — that never changes. The extra dots are drawn between them: small markers spaced evenly along each bone, plus sideways lattice lines webbing the fingers into a mesh, plus a ring of tick marks around each joint.

It's easier to see a twist or a bend in a dense mesh than in a bare stick figure. The Detail control changes only what you see — recording and training always use the original 21 points.
